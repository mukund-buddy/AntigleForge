How it works

        
Work backwards, remember forwards

      


      

        

          

            
Anchor the exam date

            
Every revision is counted backwards from the exam. The first entry in the calendar is the earliest session (the largest number of days before), and the list ends on the exam day itself as a final sweep.

          

        

        

          

            
Growth of intervals

            
The interval list — 1, 3, 7, 14, 28 by default — is widened to make the memorable pattern. You see a topic one day later, three days later, a week, two weeks, and a month out.

          

        

        

          

            
Topics travel together

            
Each row covers every topic you listed on that day. With many topics and little time, switch to a shorter pattern (like 1-3-7-14) so each subject still gets several passes before the test.

          

        

      

    

  


  
  

    

      

        
Questions

        
Revision planner FAQ

      

      

        

          What do the interval numbers mean?
          
Each number is how many days before the exam session happens. With 1,3,7,14,28, every topic gets a session one day, three days, a week, two weeks and a month before the exam. You can swap the preset for a tighter one with the select box.

        

        

          Why rehearse the same topic so many times?
          
That is spaced repetition and it turns short-term memorising into lasting recall. Revisiting stretches the memory and each session lands at the point where it would otherwise fade. Five passes beat a single all-night cram for a real retention.

        

        

          What if the exam date is very close?
          
The planner warns when it begins one to two days before the exam and keeps the pattern that best fits the days left. Choose a tighter interval (e.g. 1,3 or 1,2) to at least keep every subject warm up to the final session.
