How it works

        
Fairness by weight, freshness by rotation

      


      

        

          

            
Weights set the number of slots

            
A subject with weight 3 is placed three times as often as one with weight 1. The planner turns your list into a weighted pool and rotates it day to day, so the pattern never sticks — Chemistry is not doomed to Monday morning forever.

          

        

        

          

            
One subject per session

            
Every day reads one subject per session, and a subject never runs twice in the same day. If your list is too small to fill every slot uniquely, the leftover sessions still land by rotation instead of piling onto one subject.

          

        

        

          

            
Totals keep you honest

            
The output sums minutes per subject, so the week check is easy: did the subject you feared actually get the hours you promised? Copy the plan text into your notes and follow it.

          

        

      

    

  


  
  

    

      

        
Questions

        
Study planner FAQ

      

      

        

          How do I give some subjects more time?
          
Type the subject followed by a colon and a number, like Mathematics: 3 or Chemistry = 2. The number is how many slots that subject gets while a plain line counts as one. Weights over 6 are capped to keep the week sensible.

        

        

          Why is every subject allowed only once per day?
          
Focus runs dry when the same topic gets repeated within a couple of hours. The planner spreads variety instead: each session is a different subject, which is kinder to memory — and to morale — than three sessions of the same book back to back.

        

        

          Does the planner know my real timetable?
          
It knows no classes or hobbies you omitted — it only rebalances the list you give it. Add your harder subjects with higher weights and a schedule that matches an honest week, and the rotation stays reasonable.
